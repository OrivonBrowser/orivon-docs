# Names and content you can verify

When you open a `.eth` name or an `ipfs://` address, Orivon does not take a server's word for what
you get. It checks the answer on your machine. Servers still deliver the data; none of them is
believed.

## `.eth` names

A `.eth` name is resolved by [Helios](https://github.com/a16z/helios), an Ethereum light client
that runs inside Orivon. It follows the chain from a checkpoint shipped with each release and proves
the name's record against it. No RPC provider is trusted to tell the truth.

Addresses ending in `.eth.limo` or `.eth.link`, which other browsers reach through a gateway, open
in Orivon as the real `.eth` name.

## IPFS and IPNS

`ipfs://` and `ipns://` addresses load block by block, and every block is hashed against its
content identifier (CID) before it is used. DNSLink records are followed. Gateways are trusted to
deliver bytes, never to be right about them.

## What a server can and cannot do

| A server can | A server cannot |
|---|---|
| Refuse to answer, or answer slowly | Hand you a wrong name record unnoticed |
| See which name or content you asked for | Hand you a wrong byte unnoticed |

In short: **servers can withhold, not lie.** The root of trust is the chain checkpoint that ships
with each release.

## What the servers learn

Verification is about correctness, not anonymity. The servers that answer still see what they are
asked:

- the RPC servers learn the `.eth` names you open;
- the IPFS gateways learn the content you fetch;
- for some names, a DNS-over-HTTPS resolver or the name's own resolver learns the name.

**Settings > Web3** lists every server involved and what each one learns.

## When the light client runs

It starts when you open or type a `.eth` address and stops about ten minutes after the last one.
It also runs once shortly after launch when its newest checkpoint is more than seven days old.

**Settings > Web3 > Verify .eth names on this computer** switches it off, from the next start.
With it off, no `.eth` name loads: Orivon does not fall back to trusting a server.

## What it proves for you

Every verified load feeds the [Web3 Score](/docs/using/web3-score) in the address bar, so you can
see at a glance whether a page came from somewhere you can check.
